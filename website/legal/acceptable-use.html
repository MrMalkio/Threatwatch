Acceptable Use Policy

Threatwatch exists to reduce browser harm. Do not turn a safety tool into an attack tool, infringement tool, spam system, or reputation-manipulation system.

Effective: September 5, 2026Applies to hosted and community features
PrivacyTermsAcceptable useSafety

1. Use Threatwatch for protection, testing, and legitimate research

You may use Threatwatch to protect your own browser, study behavior you are authorized to test, create harmless fixtures, report technical issues, and improve the open-source project.

2. Do not attack third parties

Do not use Threatwatch, its rules, event data, future APIs, or community information to probe systems without authorization, deliver malware, steal credentials, evade security controls, launch denial-of-service attacks, automate abusive traffic, or compromise another person's browser, account, device, or service.

3. Do not use Threatwatch to bypass access controls

Do not use the project to bypass DRM, paywalls, authentication, subscription controls, geographic restrictions, or other access controls in violation of law or third-party rights.

4. Do not use community features to facilitate infringement

Do not submit or publish direct media files, illegal copies, access credentials, circumvention instructions, or a ranked directory whose purpose is to help people find replacement unauthorized streaming domains.

5. Do not manipulate domain reputation

Do not create fake identities, coordinated votes, automated submissions, paid ratings, false threat reports, or domain-operator campaigns designed to raise or lower a site's reputation. Paid status must never increase vote weight.

6. Keep submissions factual

Describe what the browser did, what Threatwatch observed, and what you experienced. Do not knowingly publish false accusations about people or operators. Do not include private personal data, authentication material, or information you are not entitled to disclose.

7. No live malware in public reports

Do not attach or link live malware, weaponized proof-of-concept code, stolen data, credentials, or exploit payloads in public issues. Use the private security-reporting path for a vulnerability in Threatwatch itself.

8. Enforcement

Threatwatch may rate-limit, reject, hide, quarantine, remove, or preserve for abuse investigation a submission that violates this policy. Repeat abuse can result in suspension from hosted community features if accounts are introduced.

9. Appeals

Future moderation systems should provide a reasonable way to challenge a rejected report or disputed domain record without revealing other users' private data or Threatwatch's abuse-detection methods.
